04 / design justification

why it looks, moves and reads this way.

concept

The plate

Everyone understands a plate. A full plate stands for a basic need being met.

RENT, BILLS and GROCERIES slam in one by one and stack into a mountain. Each impact pushes the plate lower and knocks food off. One broccoli is left, and the question becomes the campaign’s name.

type

Kinetic typography lets words carry the story without images, and it still works with the sound off, which is how most people watch social video.

Rent bills groceries

Anton. Heavy and condensed: big words fit a narrow vertical frame and read like a protest poster.

Canadians experienced food insecurity.

Inter. Clear at small sizes, on phones and in captions.

GROCERY RECEIPT

Space Mono. Reads like a till printout, so price data feels like an everyday bill.

colour

Warm and muted. We avoided alarm red on purpose: terracotta, the colour of tomatoes and bread crust, marks the data and the call to action, so the campaign asks for empathy instead of creating panic.

visuals

Ten flat food icons on one 100 × 100 grid, with one outline weight and one highlight each, plus the plate and the receipt. [We redrew all icons ourselves in Illustrator.] No stock images and no photos of people in need.

visualization 1

24 out of 100

A grid of 100 “O”s; each O reads as an empty plate. Twenty-four fill terracotta one by one while the number counts from 0 to 24.

“24 out of 100” instead of “24%”, because a frequency is easier to picture as people. The subtitle stays on screen from the first frame, so the number always has its meaning.

data: Canadian Income Survey 2024, via PROOF (2026)

visualization 2

The grocery bill keeps growing

A ranked dot plot printed on a grocery receipt, everyone’s most familiar record of what food costs. Rows type out like a till, each dot slides to its price change, and the largest is circled. “GROWING” stretches taller as it appears.

data: Statistics Canada, Consumer Price Index annual review 2025 (annual average change by category)

05 / reflection

what can a campaign fix, and what can’t it?

the core issue

Charity or change?

Donations relieve hunger today but do not raise incomes. A next version could add a policy ask.

Dignity

We show plates and words, not people in need, so those affected are not turned into objects of pity.

What one number hides

A national figure flattens differences by province, housing and community. We chose clarity over completeness.
